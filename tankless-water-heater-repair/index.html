A tankless water heater does not store hot water at all. It waits for a faucet to open, senses the flow, fires a burner or energizes its elements, and heats water as it races through a compact heat exchanger. That on-demand design is a big part of its appeal, but it packs a lot of sensors and controls into one small cabinet, and every one of them has to agree before you get hot water. When one of them objects, the unit shuts down and posts an error code. In Fort Collins, CO, the usual culprits are mineral scale coating the heat exchanger, a flow sensor that no longer registers demand, an igniter or flame rod that cannot prove the flame, and venting or gas supply problems that trip the unit's safety limits.

Our approach to tankless repair is simple: measure first, replace second. When we arrive at your Fort Collins home, we record the active code and the unit's fault history, then work through the systems behind it. Flow sensors are checked with water running to confirm they report demand correctly. Flame rods and igniters are tested electrically. Gas pressure is read at the inlet with the burner at maximum, because a line that holds steady at idle can sag badly under full load. The vent and air intake are inspected end to end, and the heat exchanger is judged by the temperature rise it achieves at a known flow. Only then do we recommend a fix, and we can show you exactly why.

The good news is that most tankless problems are very fixable. A descaling flush clears most mineral buildup, sensors and igniters are replaceable parts, and venting faults usually come down to a blocked intake or a loose joint. We carry common parts for the major brands and service both gas and electric units, so the typical repair is done in one trip. We are also honest about the harder cases: a unit fed by a gas line too small for its full BTU rating will keep throwing codes until the line is upsized, and a heat exchanger damaged by scale may not be worth repairing on an older heater. And if your complaint is the cold-water sandwich, that brief burst of cold between two hot draws, we will tell you whether it is normal for your model or something we can reduce. Warning Signs to Watch For

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • The unit clicking or trying to light several times before giving up
  • Leaks under the unit or around its water connections
  • No hot water at a low-flow faucet even though the shower works fine
  • A fault code on the front panel and no hot water
  • A combustion fan that sounds louder or rougher than normal
  • Outlet temperature that surges and sags while water is running
  • A cold-water sandwich that has gotten noticeably worse
  • Lukewarm water whenever two fixtures run at the same time

Why Professional Tankless Water Heater Repair Matters

From the outside a tankless heater looks like a plain box; inside, it is a tightly controlled system. One fault code often has several possible causes, and the sensors, igniters, and control boards involved are not cheap to buy on a hunch. Gas models add real hazards: the burner can fire at close to 200,000 BTU per hour, the vent carries combustion gases that contain carbon monoxide, and a loose gas connection or a vent joint that separates can put fuel or exhaust into the living space. Electric tankless units carry their own risk, often drawing well over 100 amps across several 240-volt circuits, so a loose lug or undersized wire can overheat quickly.

Professional service is usually the cheaper path, too. Our technicians do not guess at error codes. They test flow sensors with water moving, measure flame rod signal, confirm igniter resistance, read inlet gas pressure at full fire, and check the vent from end to end. When scale is the problem, we flush it out properly and rinse the system before the unit goes back in service. And because some tankless problems, like an undersized gas line or a cracked heat exchanger, are not solved by parts at all, we tell you so directly, before you commit to anything.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

A few preventive steps can help you avoid a cold shower or a wet floor down the road. Here are our recommendations:

  • Leave the power on in cold weather, since freeze protection only runs when the unit has electricity
  • Treat a code that returns after a reset as a symptom worth diagnosing, not ignoring
  • Clean the inlet water filter screen a few times a year so the flow sensor gets full flow
  • Schedule a descaling flush every year; hard water may call for one every six months
  • Tell us before adding a gas appliance, so the gas line can be checked for the extra load
  • Consider a water softener or scale-reduction filter ahead of the heater if scale keeps returning
  • Keep the vent termination and air intake clear of leaves, nests, snow, and lint
