Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Greeley

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, lift the T&P valve lever annually to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on descaling once a year in hard-water areas.

How old is your water heater? The rating plate on the tank usually includes the manufacture date, and that date says a lot about what comes next. Most tank heaters reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units about 20. If your heater is beyond that point and is also showing rusty water, rumbling, or dampness under the tank, it's usually due for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than a rushed emergency install after a leak.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Consider a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a short, inexpensive repair, but once it's gone, the water begins corroding the tank steel, and just like that a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a flooded utility room.
