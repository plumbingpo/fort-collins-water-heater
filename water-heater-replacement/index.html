Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Loud rumbling or popping that flushing no longer fixes
  • Operating costs climbing on an older, less efficient heater
  • Visible corrosion around the pipe connections or burner area
  • Rust-colored hot water while the cold stays clear
  • Less and less hot water, even after repairs
  • A tank at or past the eight-to-twelve-year mark
  • Repeated service calls on the same aging heater
  • A slow leak or damp spot under the tank itself

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Tankless units need a yearly descaling, especially where the water is hard
  • Keep the space around a heat pump model clear so it can draw heat from the room
  • Have the anode rod checked around year three to five and replaced if it is worn
  • Keep the temperature near 120 degrees for safety and slower mineral buildup
  • Note the install date and keep up with maintenance so the next replacement is never an emergency
  • Drain a few gallons every six months so sediment never gets a foothold
  • Make sure an expansion tank is in place if your home has a closed plumbing system
