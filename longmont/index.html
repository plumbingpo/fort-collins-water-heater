Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Longmont

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which reduces the risk of scalding and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on descaling once a year in hard-water areas.

The age of your heater plays a big role. With regular service, a tank water heater typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like rust-colored water, popping noises, or moisture under the tank, it's smart to start planning a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule is cheaper and is far less stressful than scrambling for a new heater with water spreading across the floor.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Take a spent anode rod: replacing it is a short, inexpensive repair, but once it's gone, the water starts eating the tank itself, and just like that a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to save our number in their phone and reach out at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and spares you a surprise cleanup.

What Goes Wrong With Local Water Heaters

Every type of water heater has its own weak points, and we work on all of them in Longmont. Gas tanks tend to have trouble with pilot assemblies and thermocouples, sooty burners, and venting that doesn't draft. Electric tanks burn out heating elements and thermostats, especially when sediment buries the lower element. Tankless heaters throw error codes for scale buildup, ignition faults, and flow sensor trouble, most of which trace back to skipped descaling. Heat pump water heaters need clean air filters and a clear condensate drain, and they struggle in cramped closets. Our technicians work on all four every week and carry the parts to fix most problems on the first visit.
